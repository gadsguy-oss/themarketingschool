What's already working

You've built the hard parts of a gifting business and shelved them.

This isn't warm-up. Each of these is genuinely difficult, and each one is a prerequisite for the thing I think you're leaving on the table.

Delivery-date selection at checkout, on live product

Your checkout lets the buyer pick the arrival date. For flash-frozen goods that's routine. For banded live lobster shipped UPS Next Day Air out of Saco, it's an inventory and logistics problem most shippers simply refuse to solve.

Date control is the single feature gifting depends on. A gift that shows up on the wrong day isn't a gift, it's a problem for the recipient.

Your reviewers describe occasions. Your site describes groceries.

From the recent reviews: a husband's birthday, a wedding anniversary, a congratulations gift for a couple with a new baby, a grandmother introducing an 8-year-old to lobster, a Fourth of July treat, a New Englander stranded in Arizona. One reviewer writes that instead of a standard gift, they wanted to send a memory.

Your customers are already telling you what business you're in. The site copy answers “buy live lobster online.”

The cooking library is a gifting asset filed under content

How to cook live lobster. How to crack one. How to butterfly a tail. How to shuck an oyster. That library is a real investment and it reads well.

It also answers the number-one objection to sending live seafood as a gift — the fear that the recipient won't know what to do with a box of live animals. Right now it lives under Cooking, not next to the gift.

A working AOV ladder

$40 flat shipping under $150, free overnight at $150, two free 4–5 oz tails at $275. Three rungs, clearly posted, no promo code friction.

Most DTC food brands are still arguing about whether to have a threshold. You have a ladder, and it's already doing basket-size work before any ad runs.

The gap

Demand is migrating out of “delivery” and into “gift.” You're positioned on the wrong side of it.

ConfirmedOver five years, the two curves crossed and never went back.

In August 2021, lobster delivery registered 43 and seafood gift registered 13 — delivery was more than three times the gift term. Averaged by year (Aug–Jul), delivery ran 35.6 → 24.6 → 21.4 → 17.7 → 21.8. Gift ran 20.2 → 19.9 → 20.2 → 21.2 → 44.2.

Delivery is down roughly a third from its 2021–22 level, and was down by half at its 2024–25 trough. Gift sat flat for four straight years and then doubled. The lines crossed for good around November 2024 and gift has led in all but a handful of months since.

Both halves matter. This isn't only a story about gifting rising — it's also a story about the transactional head term you're built around slowly draining.

ConfirmedDecember is the most reliable pattern in the whole dataset. Five years, five for five.

Ratio of December seafood gift interest to that year's September–October floor:

2021: 3.9×  ·  2022: 4.0×  ·  2023: 4.6×  ·  2024: 4.4×  ·  2025: 5.9×

The number that actually sets your calendar, though, is November: it already runs 1.5× to 2.2× the autumn floor before December arrives. The climb starts in November, which means the audiences and quality score have to be built in September and October — at September and October CPMs, not Black Friday ones.

WatchThe last twelve months broke the historical pattern. I'd treat this as a signal, not a certainty.

November 2025 came in at 38 — the highest November in the series. December 2025 hit 100, the all-time maximum of the entire five-year export. Then it didn't come back down: February 54, April 66, May 64, June 57. Each of those months individually exceeds every previous December in the dataset.

Then July 2026 dropped to 27, back near the historical norm. One month is not a reversal and eight months is not a law. Two readings are available: the category genuinely became year-round rather than a December event, or something shifted in how these queries get sampled and classified. I can't distinguish between them from a Trends export, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. Either way it's the largest movement in five years and it's pointing at the term you aren't built for.

ConfirmedYour branded search confirms the same window from the other direction.

In the weekly brand export, Maine Lobster Now sits at a median of 3 with zero in 20 of 53 weeks — a thin, noisy series, as you'd expect at your size, and I won't read meaning into the jitter down there. But three consecutive weeks break it: December 14, 21 and 28, at 8, 7 and 8. That's the only run above 5 all year and it holds both annual highs.

Two independent exports, two different scales, same window.

ConfirmedNobody in your actual competitive set owns branded search.

Get Maine Lobster: median 4. Lobster Boys: 4. The Lobster Guy: 3. All pinned to the floor alongside you. Cousins Maine Lobster runs at a median of 68 and dominates every chart it appears in, but it's a franchised food truck and restaurant brand with a Food Network show — that's people looking for a truck near them, not people shipping a box across the country. Different intent, different auction, wrong benchmark.

The honest read: this category isn't decided on brand names. It's decided on non-brand intent and on occasion, which makes paid media the whole fight rather than a supporting channel.

ConfirmedOn the site, gifting is a menu item, not a destination.

Corporate Gifts is a footer link and a sub-item under Gifts. Gift cards are product pages. Lobster Gifts is a collection. Everything exists; nothing is a campaign endpoint. The promo bar on your homepage today is a self-purchase summer tails offer running to September 4.

InferenceWhat I think is happening in the account — worth confirming.

From the outside this looks like a single Shopping feed and a Performance Max campaign serving both intents at once. If so, someone searching “seafood gift delivery” gets a product tile for a 1.5 lb live lobster at $97.98, lands on a PDP, and has to work out the gifting part themselves.

Those two intents have different searchers, different price sensitivity, different creative, different landing pages. Blended into one campaign, gifting will always look worse than it is — it converts at a lower rate on a self-purchase page, so the algorithm learns to stop bidding on it. The search terms report and the campaign structure would settle this in ten minutes; I can't see either from here.

So the gap is this: the term that's growing is the one your site treats as a footer link, the term that's shrinking is the one your whole catalog is written for, and the annual moment when both collide is decided by work done in September — not by budget dumped in on Black Friday when everyone else is bidding too.

Two terms I'd stop worrying about

lobster gift basket returns zero in 54 of 60 months, with six stray readings of 2–3. It's below Trends' reporting threshold. It sounds like an obvious keyword and it is not one — don't let anyone build a campaign around it on instinct.

What I'd do about it

Split gifting out as its own business inside the account. Start now.

Concrete enough that you could hand this to whoever runs your ads tomorrow and they'd know what to build. That's the trade — I'd rather be useful than mysterious.

Weeks 1–2
August

Separate the two intents at the account level

  • Split the product feed into a gifting feed and a self-purchase feed using custom labels. Same catalog, two personalities.
  • Pull gift-intent queries out of PMax into their own Search campaign with their own budget and bids: seafood gift, seafood gift delivery, send lobster to someone, lobster gift card, corporate seafood gifts, gift for a seafood lover, last minute food gift.
  • Add negatives so the two don't cannibalize. Gifting shouldn't bid on “how to cook lobster”; cooking shouldn't bid on “gift.”

Correction to my own first draft. Before I had the five-year export I'd have told you to bid lobster gift basket — it's the term everyone reaches for. The data says it's at zero in 54 of 60 months. Skip it. seafood gift is the head term that's actually moving.

Weeks 2–4
August

Build the gifting page you don't currently have

  • One destination that leads with the arrival date picker, not the product grid. The promise is “it shows up on the day you choose,” not “we sell lobster.”
  • Pull the cooking guides onto that page as the objection-handler: they'll know exactly what to do with it.
  • Gift card as the explicit fallback for anyone who can't confirm the recipient's schedule. That's a save, not a downsell.
  • Give Corporate Gifts a real lead path — form plus the 207 number. B2B gifting is a quoted, invoiced motion, not a Shopify checkout, and it should be measured as a lead, not a purchase.
Sept–Oct
The whole ballgame

Buy the audience while it's cheap

  • The five-year data says November is already 1.5–2.2× the autumn floor. That means the people you convert in December have to be reached in September and October, at a fraction of Q4 CPMs. Prospect now, remarket later — that's the entire trick, and it's why this is an August conversation and not a November one.
  • Build lists that matter for gifting specifically: past gift purchasers, cooking-guide readers, gift card viewers, corporate page visitors, and anyone who's ever shipped to an address that wasn't their own.
  • Get Search impression share and Quality Score accumulating on the gifting keywords now, so December bids aren't starting cold in the most expensive auction of the year.
Creative
Ongoing

Sell the arrival, not the animal

  • Your reviews describe a moment: the box at the door, the surprise, “we wanted to give them a memory.” That's the ad. A hero shot of a lobster sells a self-purchase; the doorstep sells a gift.
  • Two angles worth testing head to head — the gift for the person who has everything against the gift that arrives on the exact day you choose. My guess is the second wins on cold traffic because it's the objection, but that's a test, not a claim.
  • The Klenda family and 30 years on the Portland waterfront is a differentiator no aggregator can copy. It belongs in gifting creative, where trust is the entire purchase decision.
Measurement
Week 1

Count gift orders as their own thing

  • Flag orders where the shipping address doesn't match the billing address. That's your gifting proxy, it's already in Shopify, and it costs nothing to start tracking.
  • Report gifting and self-purchase as separate P&Ls. Blended, gifting looks like a lower-converting version of your main business. Separated, it's likely a higher-AOV one with different seasonality — but that's yours to verify, not mine to assert.
  • Watch seafood gift month over month through the autumn. If it holds above the old baseline the way it did from November through June, the year-round read is right and the budget should follow. If it keeps drifting back toward July's 27, treat it as a December business and spend accordingly.

The math

Your numbers, my framework. I've left it empty on purpose.

This is the calculation I'd run before recommending a single dollar of gifting spend. I don't know your AOV, your margin, or your target payback, so nothing is pre-filled — put your own figures in and it'll tell you what the economics actually support.

Contribution / order—Margin minus shipping. What's actually left to spend.
Break-even CAC—Above this, the order loses money on first purchase.
Break-even CVR—Conversion rate needed at that CPC just to break even.
Target CAC at 50%—Half of contribution to acquisition. A reasonable place to start.

Read this before you use the output. It's a first-purchase model — no repeat value, no gift-recipient-becomes-customer effect, which in food gifting is often the whole point. If your gift orders bring in new households at a meaningful rate, the real allowable CAC is higher than what this shows and you'd size spend off LTV instead. I'd want your repeat rate before taking any of these numbers to a budget conversation.
