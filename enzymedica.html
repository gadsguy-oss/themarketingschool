§ 02

Three things you're doing that most of the category can't

Worth saying plainly, because the argument that follows only works because these are true. If the fundamentals were weak this would be a different, much longer document.

Your differentiation survives being repeated by a stranger

Third-party reviewers explain Thera-blend unprompted — multiple enzyme strains per type, active across the full pH range of the digestive tract rather than one narrow window. That is the rarest thing in supplements: a technical claim specific enough that outsiders restate it accurately without being paid to. Hold on to this one. It becomes the whole argument in §5, because a competitor just built a demand event out of a far weaker version of it.

Observed across four independent 2026 review pages

Your content team reads culture, not a calendar

A Fiber-Maxxing guide in late July. A GlutenEase potency comparison published the morning we looked. Phytosome absorption explainers timed to the new collection. That is a team tracking what people are actually saying, at a cadence most 28-year-old supplement brands gave up on years ago. It also means the gap in §3 is a briefing problem, not a capability problem.

You already merchandise by problem, not by molecule

Shop by symptom, shop by health goal, shop by dietary need, plus two quiz funnels. Most legacy supplement brands still organise the store the way the lab organises the shelf. You've done the hard structural work of meeting people where their language is — which is exactly why the one missing occasion stands out.

§ 04

Who you're actually competing with, in five tiers

Your competitive set has quietly split. Four of these tiers you know intimately. The fifth doesn't sell supplements at all, and it's the one taking band 05.

01

Shelf peers

NOW Foods · Garden of Life · Nature's Way · Country Life · Solaray · American Health

You share aisles and retail buyers with these brands and you compete on formulation, awards and label quality — the game you've won for a decade. This tier is stable and it is not where your risk is.

Marketplace-native challengers

Physician's CHOICE · Zenwise · Nutricost · Wholesome Wellness · NatureBell · Sports Research

You started this chart four times larger than either competitor and finished it second. 2021 yearly averages: Enzymedica 10.4, Physician's Choice 2.6, Zenwise 2.6. Physician's Choice first crossed you in April 2024 and has been permanently above you since September 2024.

The April 2026 spikeAll three brands, same month

Every line on chart A peaks in April 2026 and decays together through the summer. So does digestive enzymes on chart B. Three competing brands do not spike in the same month by coincidence — something happened to the category.

It appears to be the Viral Pudding Challenge: a TikTok trend in which a Physician's Choice enzyme capsule is emptied over pudding so viewers can watch it visibly break down. Physician's Choice has since converted it into a national 30-second TV spot and club-retail activation. Their peak that month is 100. Yours is 75.

Your best search month in five years was created by a competitor demonstrating a mechanism you can demonstrate better. Thera-blend's claim is that your enzymes stay active across the full pH range of the digestive tract instead of one narrow window. That is a more interesting thing to film than a capsule on pudding, and it is defensibly yours. They made the category watchable and collected most of it. You collected the spillover.

What is confirmed and what is not. The trend, its Physician's Choice branding, the TV spot and the retail activation are all confirmed. That the trend specifically caused the April peak is an inference — the press coverage we can see postdates the peak by roughly three months, which is a normal lag but not proof. If you can date the trend's take-off internally, that either confirms or kills this paragraph, and we would want to know which.

§ 06

What we'd do about band 05

Timing first. The bloating curve in chart B bottoms in November and climbs hard from January to a June peak. It is August. Anything built now is in place before the climb; anything started in February is late. That is the whole argument for doing this in the next ninety days rather than next year.

The instinct in a supplement company is that this is a regulatory dead end. It isn't — but only if you're disciplined about which sentence you're writing. You are not claiming to treat a drug's side effects. You're naming an occasion: people are eating differently, in smaller and more protein-dense meals, and they're asking what to take with them. That's a mealtime question, and mealtime is your category.

Days 1–30

Write the page that's missing, before you spend anything

One editorial hub, built to the question people are typing, cleared by counsel line by line. Occasion language, not claim language.

  • Anchor piece on eating well on a reduced appetite — protein adequacy, fat digestion, fibre pacing.
  • Connect it to what you already sell: Lypo Gold for fat, Digest Gold for mixed meals, the fibre and probiotic lines for regularity.
  • Interlink from Fiber-Maxxing and the blood sugar and berberine posts, which are already pulling this audience without naming it.
  • Every draft gets the same test: would this sentence survive being read aloud by your regulatory lead? If not, cut it.
Days 1–30

Film the demonstration you already own

A competitor proved the category will watch an enzyme work. Your version is stronger and nobody else can run it.

  • The pH claim is the asset: same capsule, acid and alkaline conditions, one stays active. That is a visual argument, not a copy claim.
  • Shoot it as a repeatable format rather than one hero film, so creators can copy it. Trends spread because they are easy to remake.
  • Run it as organic and paid social first. If it moves, it earns the TV conversation; it does not start there.
  • Same regulatory test as everything else here: demonstrate the mechanism, claim nothing about outcomes.
Days 30–60

Buy the queries only after you can answer them

Paid traffic to a page that doesn't exist is how a brand ends up with an expensive bounce rate. Sequence matters here.

  • Tight non-brand Search cluster around the occasion and the symptom, kept away from the drug terms entirely.
  • Defensive brand coverage checked first — we can't see whether it's already solid, and it's cheap to confirm and expensive to be wrong about.
  • On Amazon, test occasion-led titles and A+ content on one SKU against your current formulation-led copy. That's the exact lever tier 02 is pulling.
Days 60–90

Decide whether it deserves a product, not just a page

You already launched a fasting formulation, so the merchandising instinct is clearly there. The question is whether the occasion supports a bundle.

  • Read the hub's conversion and assisted revenue against your existing symptom collections before committing to anything new.
  • If it holds, the obvious shape is a mealtime bundle — enzyme plus fibre plus probiotic — which is a merchandising change, not an R&D programme.
  • Re-run the Trends chart at ninety days. Share of search is the cheapest read on whether any of this moved.
