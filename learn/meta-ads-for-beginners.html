Meta ads for beginners: how to set up your first campaign

Facebook and Instagram ads can work on a small budget, but only if the basics are right. Here's how Meta campaigns are built, which settings matter, and a simple setup you can launch this week.

By Evan Baker · Updated Nov 28, 2026

Short answer: Every Meta campaign has three levels: the campaign (your objective and, often, your budget), the ad set (who sees it, where, and what Meta optimizes for), and the ad (the creative and copy). For a first campaign, pick the Sales or Leads objective, use one ad set with broad targeting and Advantage+ placements, give it enough budget to get roughly 50 conversions a week, and leave it alone through the learning phase.

How are Meta ads structured?

Meta Ads Manager organizes everything into three layers. If you understand what each layer controls, most of the settings stop being confusing.

LevelWhat you decide hereExample
CampaignThe objective (what result you want). Optionally the budget, if you use campaign-level budget."Sales: online course"
Ad setAudience, location, placements, schedule, the conversion event to optimize for, and the budget if you set it here."US, 25+, broad, Purchase"
AdThe creative (image, video, carousel), primary text, headline, call-to-action button and destination URL."Video: student result, hook v2"

One campaign can hold several ad sets, and each ad set can hold several ads. Beginners tend to build too many of both. More ad sets split your budget and your conversion data into smaller pieces, which makes it harder for Meta's delivery system to learn. A rule of thumb we use: start with one campaign, one ad set and three to five ads.

Name things so you can read them later. Something like Objective | Offer | Audience | Date at the campaign level saves you a lot of squinting in three months.

Which Meta campaign objective should you choose?

Meta currently groups campaigns into six objectives: Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion and Sales. The objective tells Meta what kind of person to find. Choose Traffic and it finds people who click. Choose Sales and it looks for people who buy. Those are often not the same people.

  • Sales: you want purchases, sign-ups or another valuable action on your website. This is the default for most businesses selling something online.
  • Leads: you want contact details, either through a form on your site or an instant form inside Facebook or Instagram.
  • Traffic: you want clicks to a page. Cheap clicks, but they rarely turn into customers on their own.
  • Engagement: likes, comments, video views, messages. Useful for specific jobs, not for sales.
  • Awareness: reach and recall. Best for brands that already have a sales engine running.
  • App promotion: installs and in-app actions.

The most common beginner mistake is choosing Traffic or Engagement because the results look cheap. Pick the objective that matches the money action. If you sell a course, run Sales and optimize for Purchase. If you book calls, run Leads or Sales optimized for the lead or booking event. That only works if your tracking is set up, so read conversion tracking basics before you launch.

What is Advantage+ and should you use it?

Advantage+ is Meta's name for its automation features. For Sales, App promotion and Leads campaigns, Meta now turns on the Advantage+ setup by default. In practice that means three settings are switched on:

  1. Advantage+ campaign budget: you set one budget at the campaign level and Meta moves money between ad sets. (This used to be called campaign budget optimization.)
  2. Advantage+ audience: you set hard limits like location, minimum age and exclusions, and Meta finds people beyond any suggestions you give it.
  3. Advantage+ placements: your ads can run across Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Threads and the Audience Network wherever Meta predicts the best results.

You can still override any of these. If you do, Meta may label the campaign as no longer fully Advantage+. That's fine. It's a label, not a penalty. The older Advantage+ shopping campaign type has been renamed and folded into Advantage+ sales campaigns, so you may still see the old name in tutorials.

For a beginner with a working pixel, our default is to leave Advantage+ on. Meta's delivery system has far more data about who converts than you do. Override it when you have a real reason, such as a legal age limit or a service area.

Audiences vs broad targeting: what actually works?

Years ago, Meta advertising was mostly about finding the right interest or lookalike audience. That has changed. With Advantage+ audience, interests and lookalikes you add are treated as suggestions. Meta starts there and goes wider if it finds better results elsewhere.

So the creative now does most of the targeting. A video that speaks to new real estate agents will mostly get shown to, and clicked by, new real estate agents, even with broad targeting. That's why people say "the creative is the targeting."

What you should still set:

  • Location: where you can actually sell or serve.
  • Minimum age: only if there's a legal or product reason.
  • Exclusions: existing customers (a custom audience from your customer list or purchasers) if you only want new buyers.
  • Language: if your landing page is in one language only.

Retargeting (showing ads to past site visitors or email subscribers) still has a place, but with a small budget you're usually better off with one broad prospecting ad set. Broad campaigns already show ads to warm people when it makes sense.

How much budget do you need for Meta ads?

Meta doesn't need a big budget, but it needs enough to get data. Work backwards from your expected cost per result.

Meta's own guidance is that an ad set generally exits the learning phase after about 50 optimization events in a 7-day period. So a useful starting budget is:

Weekly budget ≈ 50 × your expected cost per conversion

Example: if you expect leads to cost around $20, that's about $1,000 a week, or roughly $140 a day. If you expect purchases to cost $80, that's $4,000 a week, which many small businesses can't do.

If you can't afford 50 of your main conversion a week, you have three options:

  • Optimize for an earlier event that happens more often, like Add to cart, Initiate checkout or a lead, and judge results on actual sales.
  • Consolidate into fewer ad sets so the events aren't split.
  • Accept a "Learning limited" status and judge performance over a longer window. It's a warning, not a failure.

For more on picking a number you can sustain, see how much should I spend on ads.

What is the Meta learning phase and how do you get through it?

When you launch an ad set or make a significant change, Meta's delivery system goes into a learning phase. Costs swing around while it explores who responds. Ads Manager shows "Learning" in the delivery column, then "Active" once it has enough data, or "Learning limited" if it can't get enough.

What resets learning (Meta calls these significant edits):

  • Changing targeting, placements or the optimization event
  • Changing the bid strategy
  • Adding new ads to the ad set
  • Big budget jumps
  • Pausing for more than about a week

Rules we follow during learning:

  1. Don't touch it for at least 3 to 5 days unless something is broken (wrong link, tracking not firing, spending with zero results far past your target).
  2. If you raise the budget, do it gradually. A rule of thumb we use is about 20% every few days rather than doubling overnight.
  3. Batch your changes. One edit that resets learning is better than five small ones on five different days.

How do you set up your first Meta campaign, step by step?

  1. Check your tracking first. Install the Meta Pixel, set up the Conversions API if your platform supports it, and confirm your key event (Purchase or Lead) shows up in Events Manager.
  2. Create a campaign with the Sales objective (or Leads). Leave the Advantage+ setup on.
  3. Set the budget at the campaign level using the 50-conversions-a-week math above.
  4. Choose the conversion location and event in the ad set: website, and the Purchase or Lead event.
  5. Set audience controls: location, minimum age if needed, and exclude existing customers if you only want new ones. Leave suggestions light or empty.
  6. Leave placements on Advantage+.
  7. Add 3 to 5 ads that are genuinely different, not five colors of the same image. Mix formats: a short video, a static image, maybe a carousel. Write the copy using the approach in how to write ad copy.
  8. Add UTM parameters to your URLs so you can see Meta traffic in GA4.
  9. Publish and wait. Check daily that it's spending and that conversions are recording, but don't edit for the first few days.
  10. After 7 days, look at cost per result against your target and start planning your next creative test (see Meta ads creative testing).

Questions, answered

What's the minimum budget for Meta ads?

Meta lets you start with a small daily budget, but a useful budget depends on your cost per conversion. Aim for enough to get around 50 optimization events a week. If that's out of reach, optimize for a more frequent event like leads or add-to-cart, or accept a Learning limited status and judge results over a longer period.

Should I use interest targeting or broad targeting on Meta?

For most advertisers with working conversion tracking, start broad: set location, any required age limit and customer exclusions, and let your creative do the targeting. With Advantage+ audience, interests you add are treated as suggestions anyway, so Meta can go beyond them.

Is it bad if my ad set says Learning limited?

Not necessarily. It means Meta doesn't expect the ad set to reach enough optimization events to finish learning. If cost per result is acceptable, you can leave it. If not, combine ad sets, raise the budget, or optimize for an event that happens more often.

Should I boost posts instead of using Ads Manager?

Boosting is fine for getting more eyes on a post, but it gives you fewer options for objectives, conversion events and testing. If you want sales or leads, build the campaign in Ads Manager with the Sales or Leads objective.

How long should I run a Meta campaign before judging it?

Give a new ad set at least 7 days without significant edits unless something is clearly broken. Judge it on cost per conversion against your target, not on clicks or likes. See how to calculate customer acquisition cost to set that target.
