Before anything ships

Four things for the practice to verify.

Dental advertising is regulated by the New Jersey State Board of Dentistry, and platform policy restricts health-related targeting. These are flags, not findings. I am not the practice's counsel and this is not a legal opinion — each should be confirmed with the board and with whoever handles compliance.

Specialty and "specializes in" language

Cosmetic dentistry and implant dentistry are not among the recognized dental specialties in the United States. Third-party profiles for the practice carry language stating it specializes in implant and cosmetic dentistry. Board rules on how a general dentist may describe advanced training vary by state and are worth confirming — credentials held by the doctors can usually be stated accurately, but the phrasing matters, and directory profiles are frequently the copy nobody has reviewed in years.

Comfort claims and superlatives

"Painless cleanings" appears in homepage copy. Displayed patient reviews on the site include superlatives such as "best dentistry around." Comfort promises and superlatives in dental advertising commonly carry state-specific rules, including where a practice republishes patient language on its own pages.

Before-and-after photography and the advertised offer

Nineteen before/after pairs are published, along with filmed patient stories. Requirements typically cover whether images are of the practice's own patients, whether retouching is permitted, disclaimers about typical results, and patient authorization. The $350 savings offer attached to the implant pricing guide, and the free consultation referenced alongside the smile-preview tool, are advertised-offer territory with their own rules.

Health-related remarketing

Advertising platforms restrict personalized advertising based on health conditions. Remarketing lists built from visitors to specific procedure pages — the zygomatic and full-arch pages especially — can run into this. Verify current policy before building an audience strategy that depends on it. A disapproval here costs months.

Directory categorization

Not a compliance matter, but adjacent and worth the same pass: the Yelp listing for Rockaway carries the categories "Free Dental Clinic" and "Root Canal Affordable Cost." For a practice whose lead procedure is zygomatic full-arch reconstruction, those categories are recruiting exactly the wrong enquiry.
